When I joined UK Textiles as an AI Engineer in June 2026, I expected to spend my days on models. I spent them on punches, shifts and payslips — and that turned out to be the AI work.

## Models need a month you can defend

A garment unit runs on attendance. If the record of who came in, when, and on which shift is wrong, every smart feature built on top of it is wrong too. So the first job was plumbing: biometric devices syncing reliably, shift rules that judge each day with a reason, approvals that leave a trail.

Only once that record was solid did intelligence start to make sense.

## Where AI actually helps

- **Explaining, not just calculating.** Every attendance verdict carries a plain sentence: why this day was a half, why that one was full.
- **Asking the data questions.** In Zyraa, the MD assistant answers questions through read-only analytics tools, so it can look but never change a record.
- **Spotting the odd ones.** Missing punches, unusual overtime and repeated late marks are patterns worth surfacing before the pay run, not after.

## What I'd tell my student self

AI engineering in industry is mostly engineering. Learn the domain from the people on the floor, make the data honest, and keep the model on a short, read-only leash. The interesting problems show up on their own.
